# Acquired Podcast Methodology Framework

> Extracted from the 10-year anniversary episode (with Michael Lewis), host persona research, and must-listen episode analysis.

---

## Core Philosophy: "Why Has Acquired Worked?"

Acquired's success defies conventional podcasting wisdom: episodes are 4+ hours long, they release only 8-12 per year, and they rarely have guests. The formula works because they've adopted the strategies of the great companies they study.

---

## The 10 Lessons (from the 10-Year Anniversary Episode)

### 1. Scarcity Creates Value (The NFL Lesson)
- Only 8-12 episodes per year. Going from 26 → 12 → 8.
- Each episode is an EVENT, not content feed filler.
- Counter to podcasting conventional wisdom ("you have to be on all the time").
- Quote: "We release 12 episodes. The next year we're going to do eight. For the whole year."

### 2. Handcrafted Quality (The Hermès Principle)
- Every episode is "entirely handcrafted by us" — all research, all recording.
- 1,000+ edits per episode. ~300 total hours per episode (research + recording + editing).
- Audio engineer Steven Javier handles waveform editing; hosts do content editing.
- "We go in and on a transcript highlight a thousand cuts per episode."
- "It's this made-with-love product."

### 3. The Too Hard Pile (The Berkshire Lesson)
- It's OK to say no. Hollywood, The Fed, Bell Labs — all killed.
- "The answer almost always is we should just make another episode."
- Circle of competence: only do what you're genuinely great at.
- "Our opportunity cost is so high, the things we say yes to are so awesome."

### 4. True Partnership (The Sequoia/Benchmark Lesson)
- Ben and David are equal partners. No one keeps score.
- David went full-time in 2020; Ben in 2024. Equal ownership throughout.
- "It would actually break it if we ever started trying to figure out little carve-outs."
- Like Kahneman-Tversky: they bring out a better version of each other.

### 5. Independent Research + Improvised Delivery
- Research completely independently (~100 hours each). DO NOT share notes.
- "We have separate documents, we prepare separately. We try to do separate research calls."
- Improv on recording day creates genuine surprise and emotional authenticity.
- "Every episode now going into recording day feels like a high wire act."
- Added a "production meeting" one week before recording: agree on structure, not details.

### 6. The Compounding Asset (The Podcasting Advantage)
- Unlike books (Michael Lewis comparison), each episode compounds on the last.
- Subscribers are guaranteed. "People click subscribe and those people are actually subscribed."
- "We're always growing our base."
- Old episodes keep growing: "Our Costco episode, which is now two and a half years old, they just keep going."
- New episodes sell the back catalog.

### 7. Timelessness Over Timeliness
- "Everything we do must be timeless. If you listen to an episode five years later, it's 80% as relevant."
- "A CNBC article is worth 2% of its original value within a month."
- Must cover institutions that will endure for decades.
- "The best episodes are timeless AND timely" (e.g., Google series).

### 8. Reputation Over Short-Term Revenue (The Sequoia Resilience Lesson)
- When ad revenue dropped 40% in 2022, they doubled down on quality instead of chasing easy money.
- "We watched a lot of other people go for easy, secure money now. And we were just like, party's over."
- Cut sponsors to only the best partners. Eliminated low-quality content.
- "All that matters is the late years of compounding."

### 9. The Hero's Journey + Secret in Plain Sight
Three criteria for a great episode:
1. **Compelling hero protagonist** — going from obscurity to ubiquity
2. **A secret hiding in plain sight** — people know it exists but don't understand how it works (e.g., Costco's "gears turning")
3. **Important in the world** — worthy of the "Acquired stage"

### 10. Emotion + Genuine Discovery
- The hosts' emotional response teaches the audience how to feel.
- Michael Lewis: "One of your secret sauces is emotion."
- Ben: "I have to remember and bottle up my excitement of learning it in that moment to share that enthusiasm."
- Two types of notes: "I just figured out how something works" and "I just made a connection."

## Research Methodology

### Phase 1: Canonical Work (Spider Web)
- Start with the best existing work on the subject.
- "Who's done great canonical work in the past? What ideas do you have from reading it?"
- Then spider out: YouTube videos → references in those videos → audiobooks → Kindle.
- "The spider web always starts with the pre-existing canonical work."

### Phase 2: Primary Source Calls
- Since ~2023, phone calls to primary sources have become "the most important way we research."
- 25-40 calls per major episode (Google was ~40 over the year).
- Approach: "What is most misunderstood? What incorrect stories are out there?"
- On background, not for attribution. Spirit of genuine curiosity.
- Call informed — "You call knowing as much as there is to know."

### Phase 3: Physical Processing
- David writes notes by hand. Multiple cycles through material.
- "Everything needs to pass through multiple cycles of source material through my brain, through my fingers, onto a keyboard and recycle back through about three times."
- No AI note-taking. Physical notes in hard-copy books.
- The manual process IS the thinking.

### Phase 4: Script/Structure
- David creates 39-page narrative scripts. Ben creates 4,000-word insight documents.
- Production meeting one week before recording: agree on structure only.
- The script is a framework, not a teleprompter.

### Phase 5: Recording
- ~10 hours of recording, edited down to ~4 hours.
- Genuine surprise between hosts. Emotional reactions are real.
- Cold opens sometimes used for energy.

### Phase 6: Editing
- 1,000+ cuts per episode.
- Each host reviews transcript independently, cutting and rearranging.
- ~25 hours each of post-production review + ~40 hours from audio engineer.
- Aim: lowest ad-to-content ratio in the industry.

## Episode Structure Template

1. **Cold Open** — Dramatic scene-setter or hook
2. **Standard Intro** — "Welcome to season X, episode Y of Acquired..."
3. **Disclaimer** — "This podcast is not investment advice..."
4. **Sponsor Read** — Integrated, contextual
5. **The Story** — Chronological narrative with interjections
   - Origins and founding
   - Early struggles and pivot points
   - The "secret" revealed
   - Key strategic decisions analyzed
   - The climax (IPO/acquisition/dominance)
6. **Analysis** — Power framework, bull/bear case
7. **Lessons & Playbook** — Distilled strategic insights
8. **Carve-outs** — Personal recommendations from each host
9. **Outro** — Subscribe links, Slack community

## Key Analytical Frameworks Used

1. **Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers** — The primary lens for analyzing competitive moats
2. **Incentive Structures** — "Show me the incentives and I'll show you the outcome" (Munger)
3. **Path Dependence** — Understanding how history constrains and enables strategy
4. **N of 1 Philosophy** — Great companies define their markets, not follow playbooks
5. **Circle of Competence** — Only analyze what you deeply understand
6. **Dual-class / Ownership Structure** — How governance enables or constrains long-term thinking
7. **Founder-Market Fit** — Unique founder capabilities × market opportunity

## What NOT to Do (Anti-Patterns)

1. Don't share research before recording (kills surprise)
2. Don't chase timeliness over timelessness
3. Don't compromise quality for quantity
4. Don't do interviews that replace the core format
5. Don't take easy money that compromises brand
6. Don't script everything (kills emotion)
7. Don't cover something you can't make fun
8. Don't use AI note-taking (the manual process IS the thinking)
9. Don't have guests as the primary format
10. Don't cover topics without a "secret hiding in plain sight"
